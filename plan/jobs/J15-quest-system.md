# J15: Quest system and journal

**Milestone:** M5 · **Depends on:** J01 · **Unblocks:** J16, J17, J18, J19
**Reads first:** `design/quests_act1.md` (all of it, the data model must express every row); `design/gdd.md` §Quests

## Goal

Quests as data with objective types that cover every act 1 quest, a manager that tracks progress from EventBus signals, prerequisites and flags, rewards, repeatables, scripted-scene hooks, and a journal screen. J17 only authors data on top of this.

## Deliverables

- `game/quests/quest_data.gd` (`class_name QuestData`), `objective_data.gd` (`class_name ObjectiveData`)
- `game/quests/quest_manager.gd` (`QuestManager` autoload)
- `game/quests/quest_trigger.tscn` and `quest_trigger.gd`: an Area3D that emits `marker_reached(id)` for the player
- `game/ui/journal/journal.tscn`
- `game/data/quests/test_chain/*.tres`: a three-quest test chain
- `game/test/test_quests.tscn`
- GUT tests in `game/test/unit/test_quests.gd`
- New `EventBus` signals: `marker_reached(id: StringName)`, `quest_started(quest_id)`, `quest_objective_progress(quest_id, index, current, target)`, `scene_requested(scene_id: StringName)`, `xp_gained` reused for rewards

## Spec

**ObjectiveData.** `type` enum KILL, BOSS, COLLECT, REACH, DELIVER, LIGHT_CAIRNS, `description`, `target_count`, and filters: `enemy_id`, `enemy_animal`, `enemy_category`, `zone_id`, `boss_id`, `item_id`, `marker_id`, `npc_id`. `hidden_until_previous: bool` (objectives in order). `on_complete_flag: StringName` and `on_complete_scene: StringName` per objective so a mid-quest event (fog on the path home) can fire when one objective finishes.

**Counting rules.** KILL counts `enemy_died` where every set filter matches `enemy.data`. BOSS completes on `boss_defeated(boss_id)`. COLLECT tracks the inventory count of `item_id` live (progress can go down if the player drops items). REACH completes on `marker_reached(marker_id)` or on `zone_entered(zone_id)`. DELIVER completes when the player talks to `npc_id` with `target_count` of `item_id`, removing the items. LIGHT_CAIRNS counts `cairn_lit` in `zone_id`.

**QuestData.** `id`, `display_name`, `phase`, `summary`, `objectives: Array[ObjectiveData]`, `giver_npc` (empty means AUTO, the player's own question), `turn_in_npc` (empty means auto-complete when objectives finish), `prerequisite_flags`, `prerequisite_quests`, `rewards` `{xp, silver, item_ids: Array, flags: Array}`, `on_start_flags`, `on_complete_scene`, `repeatable: bool`, `auto_start_marker` (AUTO quests start when the player reaches this marker or enters this zone, if prerequisites hold).

**QuestManager.** Loads every `QuestData` under `game/data/quests/` at startup. State per quest: NOT_AVAILABLE, AVAILABLE, ACTIVE, READY_TO_TURN_IN, COMPLETED. `start(id)`, `progress(id, index, amount)`, `try_turn_in(id, npc_id) -> bool`, `complete(id)` applies rewards (xp through `xp_gained`, silver and items into the inventory, flags through `GameState.set_flag`), emits `quest_completed`, requests `on_complete_scene` through `scene_requested`, and resets repeatables to AVAILABLE. `available_for_npc(npc_id)` and `turn_ins_for_npc(npc_id)` for J16. Listens to every relevant EventBus signal once and dispatches to active quests. Auto-start logic checks `auto_start_marker` on `marker_reached` and `zone_entered`.

**Journal.** Toggled by `journal`. Two tabs: active and completed. Each active quest shows its summary and objectives with progress "3 / 8". AUTO quests are labelled as questions in the player's own words (use the `summary`). Palette UI signals.

**Test chain.** Quest A: kill 3 test dummies (KILL by `enemy_id`), auto-complete, flag `a_done`. Quest B: requires `a_done`, COLLECT 2 of a test item, DELIVER to a test NPC placeholder (a box with an npc_id that calls `try_turn_in` on interact). Quest C: repeatable, REACH a marker.

**Tests.** Filters match and don't match, COLLECT decreases on drop, prerequisites gate availability, repeatable resets, rewards apply, objective order with `hidden_until_previous`, per-objective flag fires exactly once.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The test chain plays through in the test scene with the journal reflecting each step
- [ ] Every objective type listed above is exercised by the test chain or a unit test
- [ ] A quest with an empty turn-in completes the moment its last objective finishes; one with an NPC waits for the talk
- [ ] Flags set by quests are visible in `GameState` and `flag_set` fires
- [ ] GUT tests pass, editor loads clean

## Out of scope

Real quests (J17), dialogue (J16), HUD tracker (J18).

## Notes for later jobs

- J16's dialogue asks `QuestManager.available_for_npc` and `turn_ins_for_npc` to build its offer and turn-in lines.
- J17 listens to `scene_requested` in its scene runner.
- **J16, the calls.** Both lists are `Array[QuestData]`. Accept is `start(quest.id)`. A turn-in line is `try_turn_in(quest.id, npc_id)`: it returns `true` only when that call completed the quest (then show `quest.rewards`); a delivery in the middle of a quest takes the items and returns `false`. `turn_ins_for_npc` lists a quest only while the call would do something: its objectives are finished and the NPC is its turn-in NPC, or the player holds what an open DELIVER objective for the NPC asks for. Quests have no use for `npc_talked`.
- **J16, the inventory.** J16 is the first job that has both J06's `Inventory` and this one, so it binds them: a subclass of `QuestInventory` (`game/quests/quest_inventory.gd`) that overrides `count_of`, `add`, `remove` and `add_silver` to go through the `Inventory` and emits `changed` whenever the `Inventory` changes, assigned once to `QuestManager.inventory`. Until then COLLECT, DELIVER, reward items and reward silver use the in-memory stand-in, which nothing else reads. A reward item reaches `add` as an id and nothing more, so the adapter decides its level and rarity; the quest table's "fine", "rare" and "legendary item" rewards have no field of their own, and how to say them is left open for J06 and J17.
- **J16, the test chain.** `data/quests/test_chain/` uses the NPC id `test_npc` (gives A and B, takes B's items), the enemy id `test_dummy`, the item id `test_item`, the reward item id `test_token`, and for quest C the markers `test_c_start` and `test_c_goal`. `test/quests/` holds the stand-in dummy, pickup and NPC scenes that `test/test_quests.tscn` uses.
- **J17, starting.** A quest cannot be started while `GameState.phase` is below its `phase`. The manager only reads `GameState.phase`; J17 raises it (on `flag_set` of `phase_2` and `phase_3`, say). A quest without a giver starts at its `auto_start_marker`: when the player walks into the `QuestTrigger` with that marker id, when `zone_entered` carries that zone id, and also when a flag is set or a quest completes while `GameState.current_zone` is that zone. The last covers Q12 (both flags, in Tjärnäs) and Q07 (give `vittror_2` a final SET_FLAG step and make that flag a prerequisite). A marker start needs the walk-in to come after the prerequisites. A quest with a giver never starts by itself.
- **J17, objectives.** An objective with `hidden_until_previous` counts nothing until the one before it is finished: a boss killed early is not counted later, so hide a BOSS objective only when the boss cannot be met before (Q04's Alpha Wolf). COLLECT counts what the inventory holds and takes nothing; to hand items over, follow it with a DELIVER objective with `hidden_until_previous`, one per item id (Q10 has two). When the last objective is a DELIVER to the quest's turn-in NPC, one talk delivers and completes. A REACH objective is also finished at once when it opens while the player stands in its zone. A marker has no such catch-up: a player who already stands in the trigger when the objective opens has to step out and in, so keep a trigger away from where the objective before it finishes.
- **J17, markers.** REACH with a `marker_id` and marker starts need a `QuestTrigger` (`quests/quest_trigger.tscn`, exports `marker_id` and `size`, no visual) at the place. J13's quest markers are `Marker3D`s and its `Q04_FogTrigger` a plain `Area3D`, so put a trigger there with the marker's name as its id.
- **J17, data files.** `test_chain/*.tres` show the file format. In a `.tres` the kill filters are ints: `enemy_animal` BOAR 0, WOLF 1, BEAR 2, RAVEN 3, SQUIRREL 4, FOX 5, HUMAN 6, TROLL 7; `enemy_category` NEUTRAL 0, POSSESSED 1; left out means any. A quest file with a problem (no id, a BOSS objective without `boss_id`, a giver together with an `auto_start_marker`, a prerequisite quest that does not exist) is named in an `ERROR` line at startup, which the headless checks catch. There is no on-start scene field: Q01's spawner and Q13's `farewell` hang on `quest_started` or `on_start_flags`. Objective and quest hooks fire once per run of a quest.
- **J18.** `ui/journal/journal.tscn` is a full-rect `Control` that opens and closes itself on `journal`; nothing instances it outside the test scene yet, so put it under the HUD layer. It colours itself in `Journal._apply_palette()` with its own copies of two style boxes, which the restyle can replace with the HUD theme. The tracker reads `get_active_quests()` (oldest first, so the last one is the most recent), `is_objective_open`, `get_objective_count` and `ObjectiveData.get_target()`, and redraws on `quest_updated`. `QuestData.is_auto()` tells a question from a given quest for the "New question" toast.
- **J19.** `QuestManager.to_dict()` and `from_dict(data)` are the hooks: the quests under way with their objective counts, and how often each quest was completed, as data that survives JSON. Restore `GameState` and the inventory first, because COLLECT objectives read the inventory as they come back. `reset()` forgets all progress for a new game.
- **J07.** `xp_gained(amount: int)` is already on the bus. Kills are read by name from `enemy.data.id`, `.animal`, `.category` and from `enemy.zone_id` (an empty zone counts as `GameState.current_zone`). `ObjectiveData.Animal` and `ObjectiveData.Category` repeat EnemyData's two enums value for value, plus `ANY = -1`; a test in `test_quests.gd` compares them from the day `EnemyData` exists.
- **J09 and J11.** Once the bus declares `boss_defeated(boss_id: StringName)` and `cairn_lit(cairn)`, the manager connects to them by name at startup; nothing here needs changing. A cairn that has a `zone_id` counts in that zone, any other in the zone the player is in.
- **J12.** Set `GameState.current_zone` before emitting `zone_entered`, as the J12 spec has it: starts and REACH objectives that depend on where the player already is read it.
- **Every job that adds a listener to the bus.** J15's GUT tests speak on the real `EventBus`: `enemy_died` with a stand-in enemy (a `Node3D` in the scene that has `data` and `zone_id` and nothing else of an `Enemy`), and `zone_entered`, `marker_reached`, `flag_set`, `scene_requested`, `xp_gained`, `quest_completed` with ids that start with `test_` or are otherwise not act 1's. A listener that comes later has to let those pass: J17's scene runner ignores a scene id it has no data for, and J19's autosave is not meant to write a save from inside a unit test.
- **J22.** `QuestManager.start(id)` starts only a quest that is AVAILABLE (set its flags or the phase first); `complete(id)` forces any quest that is under way. The test chain is loaded in every build, since it lives under `data/quests/`; nothing in the real game has its NPC or its markers.

## Deviations

- **Signals.** All five new signals carry typed parameters (the J01 note). `xp_gained(amount: int)` is declared here because J07, which owns it, is not merged; J07 finds it on the bus. The comments of `quest_updated` and `quest_completed` were reworded to what the manager does: `quest_updated` follows every change of a quest under way, and `quest_completed` also fires for a quest without a turn-in NPC.
- **`boss_defeated` and `cairn_lit` are not declared here.** They belong to J09 and J11, which are not merged, and a declaration here would collide with theirs when they are. The manager connects to each by name if the bus has it (`LATER_BUS_SIGNALS`) and exposes `notify_boss_defeated(boss_id)` and `notify_cairn_lit(cairn)`, which the tests call. One test adds both signals to the bus for its own length and checks that a manager hears them.
- **The inventory is a stand-in.** J06 is not merged, so `QuestManager.inventory` holds a `QuestInventory` (`game/quests/quest_inventory.gd`): counts per item id and a silver sum in memory, with `count_of`, `add`, `remove`, `add_silver` and `changed`. J16 swaps in an adapter over the real `Inventory` (Notes for later jobs).
- **Enemies and cairns are read by property name** (`data.id`, `data.animal`, `data.category`, `zone_id`), because J07 and J11 are not merged. `enemy_animal` and `enemy_category` are the enums `ObjectiveData.Animal` and `ObjectiveData.Category`, which repeat J07's spec order with `ANY = -1` as the unset filter.
- **`rewards` is a `QuestRewards` sub-resource** (`game/quests/quest_rewards.gd`) with `xp`, `silver`, `item_ids` and `flags`, not a Dictionary, so the four fields are typed like everything else. An id named twice in `item_ids` gives two of the item.
- **`phase` gates a quest:** it cannot be started while `GameState.phase` is lower. `design/quests_act1.md` puts every main quest in a phase and says the repeatables are "available from phase 1", and no other field could say that Q05 belongs to phase 2.
- **States.** Only quests under way are stored. NOT_AVAILABLE and AVAILABLE are worked out from the prerequisites whenever `state_of` is asked, so no signal announces them. A repeatable quest is counted (`times_completed`) each time it completes; it counts as a prerequisite and is listed in the journal's completed tab once it has completed at least once.
- **`start(id) -> bool` starts only an AVAILABLE quest. `complete(id) -> bool` completes any quest that is under way,** whatever its objectives say, so that J22's console can force one. `try_turn_in` returns `true` only when the quest completed in that call.
- **Counting.** Each event that matches adds one, also for BOSS and REACH, which finish at `target_count` 1. The event that finishes an objective is not counted for the objective it opens. COLLECT shows at most its target. `progress()` does nothing for a COLLECT objective, which follows the inventory.
- **`hidden_until_previous` is read live:** an objective is open while the one before it is finished, so it closes again if that one is a COLLECT whose items leave.
- **DELIVER happens in `try_turn_in`,** the call the spec gives the test NPC, not on a signal. It takes the items, and the quest's COLLECT objectives for that item that were finished stay finished instead of following the inventory down; without that a quest of COLLECT then DELIVER could never complete.
- **Zones.** The zone of a kill or a lit cairn is the node's `zone_id`, or `GameState.current_zone` when it has none. A REACH objective for the zone the player already stands in is finished when it opens. A quest without a giver also starts when its last prerequisite arrives while the player is in its auto-start zone; J17's Q12 and Q07 are described that way. A repeatable quest that completes in its own auto-start zone does not restart from that completion, which would never end.
- **Objective hooks fire once per run of a quest,** also for a COLLECT objective that is finished, unfinished and finished again; the spec's test asks for the flag "exactly once".
- **Quest data is checked when it is loaded.** `QuestData.get_problems()` names what would keep a quest from working (no id, an empty objective, an objective without the filter its type needs, a giver together with an `auto_start_marker`); the manager leaves such a quest out with an error. J17 writes sixteen quest files by hand with nobody at the editor.
- **`to_dict()`, `from_dict()`, `reset()`, `load_quests()` and `register()` were added.** J19's job file reads this one for the first two; the tests use the others to give each test a new game and to add quests built in code.
- **Read access.** The spec names the calls that change a quest. The journal, J16 and J18 also have to read, so the manager has `get_quest`, `get_quests`, `state_of`, `times_completed`, `get_active_quests`, `get_completed_quests`, `get_objective_count`, `is_objective_finished` and `is_objective_open`.
- **The journal** is a `Control` with `journal.gd` (`Journal`), so the HUD layer can hold it; its panel stands at the right edge, which leaves the player in view while it is open. A quest nobody gave is listed under its `summary` with the tag "Question" and without its name; a given quest under its name with the summary below. It shows the open objectives as "description  3 / 8", finished ones fainter, and "Ready to turn in" for a quest that waits for its NPC. A completed repeatable quest shows how often it was done. It rebuilds on `quest_updated` while open.
- **`QuestTrigger` has a `size` export** and is a `@tool` script like `BoxProp`, so a trigger is sized where it is placed and the editor shows that size. It announces every entry of the `Player` body and has no visual.
- **The test chain.** A and B are given by `test_npc`, so that J16 can offer them; B is turned in to it. C has no giver: it starts at the marker `test_c_start` once B is completed (which exercises `prerequisite_quests`) and reaches `test_c_goal`. B's COLLECT objective sets `b_items_found` and C asks for the scene `test_stone_scene`, to show the hooks in the test scene's log.
- **The test scene stands in for three jobs that are not merged.** `test/quests/` holds a dummy that dies on E and respawns (J07), a pickup that goes into the stand-in inventory (J06) and an NPC box that turns in and accepts on E (J16). The 1 key drops a test item. The scene script is `test/quest_test_scene.gd` (`QuestTestScene`).
- **GUT tests beyond `test_quests.gd`:** `test_quest_trigger.gd`, `test_journal.gd` and `test_quest_scene.gd`, which plays the whole chain in the test scene and reads the journal after every step, because nobody can look at the scene.
- **`game/README.md` gained one line** under Autoloads. `game/quests/.gdkeep` and `game/ui/journal/.gdkeep` were removed; the folders are no longer empty.
- **Checked headless, not by eye.** The chain, every objective type, both ways of completing, the flags and the journal's text and colours are GUT tests; the journal's key is sent as an input event and the triggers fire from real physics, once with the player walking there on held keys. What is left for a person: how the journal and the test scene look, and one look at the editor's Output panel.
